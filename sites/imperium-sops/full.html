Rules for every job

Every job runs the same way, so the customer gets the same car back no matter who is on the tools.

Day before

  1. Send the confirm text. Put in the day, time and price range. Ask for the outdoor tap and a power point. Ask them to clear loose things out of the car.

    How to do it

    Text the customer the day before so nothing is a surprise. Put in the day, the time you'll get there and the price range. Ask them to leave the outdoor tap and a power point free, and to take loose things out of the car. No reply by the evening? Give them a quick call.

  2. Van check: water, power lead, chemicals topped up, LED light, paint gauge, polisher and clean pads, coating kit, business cards, review cards.

    How to do it

    Pack the van the night before, not in the morning. Touch each thing as you check it: water tank full, power lead, chemicals topped up, LED light and paint gauge charged, polisher and clean pads, coating kit, business cards and review cards. One missing thing on the day can cost an hour.

  3. Towels in four bags, never mixed: paint, wheels, interior, glass.

    How to do it

    Use four separate bags: paint, wheels, interior, glass. Towels never move between bags. A wheel towel carries brake dust and grit that scratches paint, and a glass towel with wax or dressing on it leaves smears.

Morning of

  1. Send the on-our-way text with a real arrival time.

    How to do it

    Send it as you leave, with a real time like "Be there at 8:40", not "this morning". Running late? Text them again straight away with the new time.

On arrival

  1. Say who you are. Confirm the job and the price out loud before touching the car.

    How to do it

    Say your name and that you're from Imperium. Then say the job and the price out loud, for example "Full detail today, at the price we sent you." Agreeing before you start stops arguments at the end.

  2. Walk around the car with the customer. Point out chips, scratches, kerbed wheels, cracks, stains and missing trim. Photo each one.

    How to do it

    Do this with the customer standing next to you. Point at every chip, scratch, kerbed wheel, crack, stain and bit of missing trim, and take a photo of each one. It protects us if they later say we caused it, and it shows them we're careful.

  3. Ask three things: anything valuable inside, child seats out or staying, any spot they care most about.

    How to do it

    Ask: "Anything valuable inside I should know about?", "Are the child seats coming out or staying in?" and "Is there one spot you care about most?" The last answer tells you where to spend extra time. That's the first spot they'll check.

  4. Check the site. Make sure the tap works and the power lead reaches the car. For correction or coating, make sure the car is under cover.

    How to do it

    Turn the tap on and check the flow. Run the power lead to the car before you start. Correction and coating need the car under cover, like a garage or carport, because sun, wind and dust ruin the finish. No cover? Text Angus before you start.

  5. Park out of direct sun if you can. Panels must be cool to touch before any chemical goes on.

    How to do it

    Hot panels dry chemicals before you can rinse them off, and that leaves spots and stains. Put the back of your hand on the bonnet and the roof. If it's too warm to keep your hand there, move the car into shade or wait for it to cool.

Always

  • Never quote below the price list. Full and interior details can have a condition charge of up to $75. Tell the customer about it before you start, never after.

    How to do it

    Quote from the price list, never less. Full and interior details only: if the car is extra dirty, like heavy pet hair, sand or mould, you can add up to $75. Tell the customer before you start and get a yes. Never add it at the end.

  • Not sure? Text Angus. Don't guess.

    How to do it

    A text costs nothing. A wrong guess can damage a car or lose a customer. If you don't know the price, the product or the method, ask first and wait for the answer.

  • Wheel towels never touch paint. A dropped towel is a dirty towel.

    How to do it

    Wheel towels carry brake dust and grit that will scratch paint, so they stay in their own bag. If any towel touches the ground, it goes in the wash bag and isn't used again on this job.

  • Test any chemical on a hidden spot first if you don't know the surface.

    How to do it

    Some surfaces stain or fade with the wrong chemical, like soft plastics, leather, matte paint and old trim. Spray a little where nobody looks, wait a minute, wipe it off and check. Looks the same? Go ahead.

  • Take the job photos the app shows for this job (up to 12): front 3/4 before, dirtiest panel, wheel before, interior before, foam on, extraction water, polisher on panel, 50/50 if correcting, wheel after, interior after, beading, front 3/4 after from the same spot as the before.

    How to do it

    These photos prove the work and feed the website and the ads. The app shows each one right at the step where you take it, and only the ones that fit this job. The full list: front 3/4 before, dirtiest panel, wheel before, interior before, foam on, extraction water, polisher on a panel, the 50/50 if correcting, wheel after, interior after, water beading, front 3/4 after from the same spot as the before.

  • Leads come first in breaks. Reply within 5 minutes.

    How to do it

    A new enquiry goes cold fast. When you stop for a break, check your phone first and reply to anything new within 5 minutes. The business that answers first usually gets the job.

Filming

Every job gets filmed. These clips become the reels, the ads and the before and afters. Each service below has its own clip list.

  • Phone vertical, 4K, 60fps. Slow-mo for foam and water.

    How to do it

    Hold the phone upright for reels. Set the camera to 4K at 60 frames a second. Use slow-mo for foam going on and water running off, because those clips look best slowed down.

  • Hold the phone still at the start and end of every clip. Film for the length in the clip list. No talking unless it's a talking clip.

    How to do it

    Hold the phone still for a moment at the start and the end. That gives clean bits to cut in the edit. In between, a slight, slow pan, smooth and never shaky, unless the clip says to hold it still. Stay quiet so music can go over it, unless the clip is meant to have talking.

  • Film the before and the after from the same spot, same angle, same direction. That's what makes the change easy to see.

    How to do it

    Before you film the before, remember exactly where you stood. Film the after from that same spot, same height, same direction. Side by side, the change is obvious.

  • Wipe the lens before every clip.

    How to do it

    Fingers make the lens greasy and water makes it misty. Wipe it with a clean microfibre before every clip. A blurry clip can't be used.

  • Cover or blur number plates before anything is posted.

    How to do it

    A number plate can identify the customer. Blur or cover it in the edit before anything goes online. Never post it raw.

  • Customer in a clip only if they say yes.

    How to do it

    Ask first: "Mind if you're in a quick clip?" Only film them if they clearly say yes. If they say no, film the car only.

  • Story videos: a slight, slow pan. Keep it smooth, never shaky.

    How to do it

    Move the phone slowly to one side while you film, like a slow turn of your body, not your wrist. Hold it with both hands, elbows tucked in. Start and stop still. A smooth slow pan looks pro; a shaky one gets skipped.

Exterior detail

Wheels first, then foam, wash top to bottom, iron remover and clay, sealant, dry. Dirt never travels back up the car.

  1. Wheels and tyres. Spray wheel cleaner, then foam. Clean in this order: barrel brush, wheel face, tyre brush, arch brush, then the small brush for corners and calipers. Rinse.

    • Dirty rims: use the acid wheel cleaner. Never acid on chrome, polished, bare metal or coated wheels.
    • Wheels bucket: 1 or 2 capfuls of Green Star, measured with the bottle's lid.
    How to do it

    Wheels go first so their dirty spray never lands on a clean car. Really dirty rims get the acid cleaner, but never on chrome, polished, bare metal or coated wheels: acid ruins them. Not sure? Use the normal wheel cleaner. Put 1 or 2 lids of Green Star in the wheels bucket. Brush in order: barrel, face, tyre, arch, then corners and calipers. Rinse well.

  2. Pre-wash. Foam the whole car. Leave it 1 to 2 minutes. Rinse off in the same order you sprayed it on.

    • Foam cannon: Green Star and the touch wash chemical, 1:3, 100 ml in total, the rest water. It neutralises any acid from the wheels.
    How to do it

    Fill the foam cannon with 25 ml Green Star and 75 ml touch wash chemical, then top it up with water. The Green Star cancels out any acid from the wheels. The foam lifts loose dirt so the mitt doesn't drag it across the paint. Let it sit 1 to 2 minutes, never let it dry, then rinse it off in the same order.

  3. Touch wash. Foam the car again with just a little touch wash chemical. No Green Star. The foam doesn't need to be thick. Grab a clean mitt. Wash top to bottom: roof, windscreen, bonnet, then down the car. Flip the mitt every few panels. Rinse the mitt in the grit-guard bucket, dip it back in the touch wash bucket, and carry on.

    • Touch wash bucket: the touch wash chemical and water. No Green Star.
    • Never go from a low panel back up high unless you're on the clean side of the mitt.
    How to do it

    This second foam is light: just a little touch wash chemical, no Green Star. It doesn't need to be thick. The touch wash bucket is the soap bucket: touch wash chemical and water. The second bucket is the grit-guard bucket: plain water with the grit guard. Wash top down in straight lines, flip the mitt every few panels, and scrub it on the grit guard before it goes back in the soap bucket.

  4. Rinse. Check the foam hasn't dried on the windows. If it has, wipe them again with the mitt straight away. Rinse the whole car until no foam is left. Every corner should be clean now.

    How to do it

    Look at the windows first. Dried foam leaves marks, so if it has dried, wipe the glass with the mitt again straight away. Then rinse from the top down until no foam is left anywhere: mirrors, badges, grilles and door edges too.

  5. Iron remover. Spray it on all the paint, and only the paint. Rinse after 1 to 2 minutes, or sooner if it starts to dry. Rinse it all off.

    How to do it

    Iron remover melts tiny bits of brake dust stuck in the paint. It turns purple when it's working. Paint only, not glass or trim. Never let it dry: on a warm day, rinse sooner or do one side at a time. Rinse every bit off.

  6. Clay. Use the clay sponge on the paint, glass and trim. Move it side to side. Every few panels, dunk it in the touch wash bucket, squeeze it, soak up fresh water and carry on. Keep going until all the bugs are gone.

    • Really stubborn bugs or tar? Bug and tar remover on them first, then clay.
    How to do it

    The clay sponge pulls out dirt that washing leaves behind, like sap and bug bits. Really stubborn bugs or tar get bug and tar remover first. Keep the surface wet and soapy and glide it side to side with light pressure. When the panel feels smooth instead of rough, it's done. Drop it on the ground? It doesn't go back on paint.

  7. Rinse the whole car fully.

    How to do it

    Rinse the whole car from the top down to wash off the clay soap and anything it lifted. The paint must be completely clean before the sealant goes on.

  8. Ceramic sealant. Wet a clean towel with the pressure washer and fold it into quarters. Spray the sealant 3 times over the towel. Spray 2 to 3 times on the panel, wipe side to side over the whole panel, then rinse straight away. Never wait to rinse. Panel by panel, whole car.

    Not on paint correction jobs.

    How to do it

    This puts a layer of protection on the paint. Wet a clean towel with the pressure washer, fold it into quarters, and spray the sealant 3 times over it. Spray the panel 2 to 3 times, wipe side to side over the whole panel, then rinse it straight away. If it sits or dries, it streaks. Panel by panel, all the way round.

  9. Dry every area. No water left anywhere, rims too.

    How to do it

    Use clean drying towels. Lay the towel flat and pull it across the panel instead of rubbing hard. Do the rims too. Any water left behind turns into spots.

  10. Blower on every corner, gap and crevice.

    How to do it

    Water hides in mirrors, badges, grilles, handles, lights and the fuel flap, then drips out later and leaves streaks. Blow every gap until nothing drips, then wipe any drips with a towel.

  11. Glass cleaned, no streaks.

    How to do it

    Glass towels only. Spray the towel, not the glass, so nothing drips onto the paint. Wipe, then buff with a dry glass towel. Check from inside and from an angle: streaks show up best against the light.

  12. Tyre shine on.

    How to do it

    Put on a thin, even coat with an applicator, not your hand. Wipe off any extra so it doesn't fling onto the paint when the car drives away. Keep it off the tread and the brakes.

  13. Walk around and look for any dirt left.

    How to do it

    Walk slowly around the whole car and crouch down low. Check the lower doors, behind the number plates, exhaust tips, the bottom of the bumpers and the wheels. Fix anything now, before the customer finds it.

  14. Dry all the door jambs.

    How to do it

    Open every door and the boot. Wipe the jambs dry so water doesn't run out onto the clean paint later. The customer sees them every time they get in.

Interior detail

Your goal the whole way through is to find something dirty and make it clean.

  1. Clear out. Put the customer's things in a clean, dry spot. Bin all rubbish.

    How to do it

    Check the console, door pockets, glovebox, under the seats and the boot if it's part of the job. Put the customer's things in a box or bag in a clean, dry spot. Only bin obvious rubbish. Not sure if it's rubbish? Keep it.

  2. Door jambs. Rinseless wash on every jamb. Get the corners and the bottoms. They should be perfect.

    How to do it

    Spray rinseless wash onto a towel and wipe every door jamb, the boot jamb and the sills. Get into the corners and along the bottom edges, where dirt piles up. They should look as good as the paint.

  3. Mats. Take them out. Drill brush them, then vacuum, to lift all the dirt. Stained mat? Spray it with water and use the extractor.

    How to do it

    Take every mat out. Drill brush them to loosen the dirt, then vacuum. For stains, spray with water and run the extractor over them until the water coming out runs clear. Leave them somewhere to dry while you do the rest.

  4. Carpets. Spray interior cleaner. Use the drill brush again. Then go over the carpets with the pet hair block in straight lines, up and down.

    How to do it

    Spray interior cleaner on the carpet, scrub it in with the drill brush, then go over it with the pet hair block in straight lines, up and down. The block pulls out hair and leaves neat lines. Damp is fine, soaking wet is not.

  5. Hard surfaces. Move the seats all the way forward and back, up and down, so you can reach everything. Spray, scrub with a brush, then wipe off. Do every hard surface.

    How to do it

    Move each seat fully forward, back, up and down so you can reach everything around it. Spray the cleaner onto the brush, scrub, then wipe it off with a clean interior towel. Dash, doors, console, seat rails, every plastic. Keep sprays away from screens and buttons.

  6. Roof handles and sun visors. Don't miss them.

    How to do it

    They get touched every day and are easy to forget. Wipe the grab handles above the doors, and both sides of the sun visors, mirrors included.

  7. Check. Look over everything. Find something dirty and clean it.

    How to do it

    Stop and look over the cabin the way the customer will. Your job is to find something dirty and make it clean. Try the cup holders, vents, seat belt buckles and pedals.

  8. Vacuum. Move the seats all the way forward and back again. Vacuum up and under the seats, between them and in the cracks of the seats.

    How to do it

    Slide the seats right forward and vacuum behind and under them, then right back and do the front. Get between the seats and the console and into every seat crease with the crevice tool.

  9. Glass. Use the red towel, then the green towel. Do 4 crosshatch passes with each. Get into the corners.

    How to do it

    Do 4 crosshatch passes with the red towel, then 4 with the green towel: side to side, then up and down. Push into the corners and along the top edge of the glass. Don't forget the mirror and the back window.

  10. Seats. Check every seat is clean.

    How to do it

    Look at each seat closely in good light. Check the seams, the sides near the door and the backs of the front seats. Clean any marks now.

  11. Final vacuum.

    How to do it

    One last vacuum over everything to pick up the dust from cleaning. Finish the carpet in neat, straight lines.

  12. Finish. Mats back in, steering wheel cover on. New customer? Put the review card and business card inside the cover or on the middle console.

    How to do it

    Mats back in and straight, steering wheel cover on. For a new customer, tuck a review card and a business card inside the cover, or leave them on the middle console. It's the first thing they see, and the cards remind them to review us and pass our number on.

Paint correction

Measure the paint before you polish. Start with the gentlest pad and polish combo that works. Never polish over grit. On a ceramic job, the sealant and finish steps are left out: the coating comes next. Under cover only. Goal: 2 to 3 hours, exterior included.

  1. Exterior done. Washed, iron remover, clay, rinsed, dried and blown out. No sealant: nothing goes on the paint before you polish.

    How to do it

    On this job the exterior steps above stop after the blower: wheels, pre-wash, touch wash, rinse, iron remover, clay, rinse, dry, blower. Just check they're all done. No ceramic sealant: the paint has to be bare when you polish. A dry car also means the tape sticks and no water runs onto the pad.

  2. Tape off rubbers, plastics, badges, panel edges and sharp body lines.

    How to do it

    Polish stains rubber, plastic and badges, and the polisher can cut through the thin paint on edges and sharp lines. Cover them all with masking tape before you start.

  3. Inspect under the LED. Mark the worst areas and photo each one. Show the customer what will come out and what won't. If your fingernail catches on a scratch, it's through the clear coat. It won't come out. Say so before you start.

    How to do it

    Shine the LED across each panel to find swirls and scratches, and mark the worst spots. Photo each one: you'll take the after from the same angle in step 11. Show the customer what will come out and what won't. Fingernail test: run a nail across the scratch. If it catches, it's through the clear coat and won't polish out. Say so now, not after.

  4. Measure paint with the gauge. Take at least 5 readings per panel, and more on the bonnet, roof and boot. Write them down. A panel is light touch if it's under about 80 microns, or has a big jump between readings (repaint or filler). Light touch means finishing polish only, or skip it. Not sure? Ask Angus.

    How to do it

    The gauge measures how thick the paint is, in microns. Take at least 5 readings on every panel, more on the bonnet, roof and boot, and enter them in Paint readings below. The app flags any panel under the minimum, or with a big jump between readings (a sign of repaint or filler). Flagged means light touch: finishing polish only, or skip it.

  5. Test spot. Pick the pad and polish to match how bad the paint is. Check the spot under the LED. Whatever works on the test spot is the plan for the whole car.

    • Cooked paint: microfibre pad, heavy cut
    • Less cooked: microfibre pad, fine cut
    • Moderate swirls: foam pad, heavy cut
    • Basically perfect: foam pad, fine cut
    How to do it

    Do a small test patch on one side of the worst panel, the side you'll polish first, so the other side stays untouched for the 50/50. Put the polish on the same way as step 6. Start with the gentlest combo you think will work, wipe it off and check under the LED. Swirls still there? Try the next stronger combo. Whatever works is the plan for the whole car. Tap the combo you used below.

  6. 50/50 on the worst panel. Run one strip of tape down the middle. Polish one side with that combo. Film the 50/50 under the LED and take its photo. Then peel off just that strip.

    • Polish on the pad: 4 to 5 pea-sized drops, north, south, east and west of the centre, or in a star shape.
    • Stamp the pad on the panel or area you're doing first. Spread it on the lowest speed, then go up to normal speed.
    • Normal speed: microfibre pad speed 4, foam pad speed 5.
    How to do it

    A 50/50 is a panel half polished and half not: our most valuable clip. Tape one line down the middle of the worst panel. Put 4 or 5 pea-sized drops on the pad, like a star. Press the pad flat on the panel before you switch on, so it doesn't fling. Spread on the lowest speed, then speed 4 (microfibre) or 5 (foam). Polish one side only, slow overlapping passes. Wipe it, then film it and take the photo under the LED. Peel off just that strip. The other tape stays on until step 10.

  7. Do the whole car with that combo, the same way, starting with the other half of the worst panel. Use the small polisher in tight and fine areas. Blow the pad clean with air often. Keep the pad off edges and body lines.

    How to do it

    Same combo, same way as the 50/50: the other half of the worst panel first, then the rest of the car. Small sections, slow overlapping passes. Blow the pad clean often, and keep off edges and body lines.

  8. Alcohol prep over the whole car to remove all the polish oils.

    How to do it

    Polish leaves oils behind that hide swirls and stop protection sticking. Wipe every polished panel with the alcohol prep on one clean towel, then buff dry with a second.

  9. Inspect again under the LED from three angles. Rework any hazing, holograms or missed spots.

    How to do it

    Check every panel with the LED from three different angles. Look for hazing (cloudy patches), holograms (wavy trails that show in light) and any spot you missed. Go over them again until they're gone.

  10. Remove tape, clean dust out of gaps and badges with a soft brush and blower.

    How to do it

    Peel the tape off slowly. Then use a soft brush and the blower to clear polish dust out of gaps, badges, grilles and trim. Dust left behind looks messy and gets under the protection.

Ceramic coating

The coating is only as good as the paint under it, so prep is most of the job. Under cover only. Goal: 4 to 5 hours on site.

Before you open the bottle

  1. Exterior prep done. The wash, iron remover and clay were done at the start, in the exterior steps, with bug and tar remover where it was needed. Any old wax or sealant must be off too.

    How to do it

    You did this already. Just check it. The wash, iron remover and clay were done at the start of the job, with bug and tar remover on anything really stubborn. Any old wax or sealant has to be gone, because the coating can't stick on top of it.

  2. Paint correction done. At least a single stage, even on a brand new car. It takes out marks from transport and dealer washes.

    How to do it

    A coating locks in whatever is under it, swirls included. So every coating gets at least a single-stage polish, even on a brand new car. That was the paint correction steps above: just check it's done. New cars often have marks from transport and dealer washes.

  3. Tape off rubbers, unpainted plastics you aren't coating, and badges.

    How to do it

    Tape off rubbers, bare plastics you're not coating, and badges. Coating is very hard to get off them once it hardens.

  4. Panel prep wipe on every surface you're coating. Use IPA or panel prep spray. Wipe with one towel, then buff dry with a second. After this, never touch the paint with bare hands.

    How to do it

    Spray IPA or panel prep, wipe with one towel, then buff dry with a second clean towel. Do every surface you'll coat. From here on, bare hands never touch the paint: skin oil stops the coating sticking.

  5. Check the conditions: under cover, out of sun and wind, panels cool, good light. Temperature and humidity must be inside the range on the bottle.

    How to do it

    Coating needs calm, cool, clean air: under cover, out of sun and wind, panels cool to touch, and enough light to spot high spots. Check the label for the right temperature and humidity. Outside that range? Don't start. Text Angus.

Applying

  1. Gloves on. Fresh applicator and suede, a stack of clean towels.

    How to do it

    Put gloves on. Use a fresh applicator with fresh suede, and have a stack of clean, dry towels ready, more than you think you'll need.

  2. Two lines of ceramic on the applicator.

    How to do it

    Squeeze two lines of coating across the suede. Add two more when it stops spreading easily. Don't flood it: a little goes a long way.

  3. 60 x 60 cm section, crosshatch: side to side, then up and down.

    How to do it

    Work in squares about 60 by 60 cm, roughly a big floor tile. Spread side to side, then go over the same square up and down. That crosshatch leaves an even coat with no gaps.

  4. Leave it 20 to 30 seconds.

    How to do it

    Wait 20 to 30 seconds. You may see a rainbow sheen, called flashing. That means it's ready to wipe. On a hot or humid day it flashes faster, so wipe sooner.

  5. Two towels. First one to buff it off, second one for the fine buff.

    How to do it

    First towel: gently wipe off the extra. Second, clean towel: a light final buff. Fold the towels often so you always use a clean side.

  6. No high spots. Look at the paint from different light angles straight away. If a high spot hardens, it has to be polished off.

    How to do it

    A high spot is coating that wasn't wiped off evenly. It looks like a dark or rainbow smear. Check each square from a few angles with the light straight away. Once it hardens it has to be polished off, so catch it now.

  7. Overlap each section slightly so there are no gaps.

    How to do it

    Start each new square slightly over the edge of the last one, so no strip of paint is left uncoated.

  8. Second layer if the package has one, after the wait time on the bottle.

    How to do it

    Some packages get a second layer. Wait the time on the bottle before putting it on. Too soon, and you disturb the first layer.

  9. Extras by package: lights and exterior plastics, glass, wheel faces, door and boot jambs, interior. Right product on the right surface.

    How to do it

    Check what the package includes: lights and exterior plastics, glass, wheel faces, door and boot jambs, interior. Use the right product for each surface: glass coating on glass, wheel coating on wheels, and so on.

  10. Final walk of every panel under the LED. Any doubt, buff it.

    How to do it

    Walk round with the LED and check every panel for high spots or smears. Not sure about a spot? Buff it. Easy now, hard later.

  11. Finish. Peel off all the tape. Then the glass and door jambs you didn't coat, tyre shine, and a walk around for any dirt left, the same as the exterior detail.

    How to do it

    The tape from before the coating is still on, and the exterior stopped before the polish. Peel the tape off slowly. Clean the glass and wipe the door jambs you didn't coat, put tyre shine on the tyres, and walk around for any dirt left. The after photos and clips come next, so the car has to look finished.

After

  1. Car stays under cover 24 hours and out of rain 48 hours. No wash for 7 days.

    How to do it

    The coating needs time to harden. The car stays under cover for 24 hours, out of rain for 48 hours, and doesn't get washed for 7 days. You tell the customer at the handover.

  2. Record for the warranty: name, car, rego, date, package, coating and bottle batch, layers, paint readings, photos.

    How to do it

    Fill in the warranty record below: name, car, rego, date, package, coating and bottle batch, layers, paint readings and photos. If there's ever a claim, this is the proof of what was done.

  3. Book the annual check in the calendar before you leave.

    How to do it

    Put the annual check in the calendar before you leave, a year from today. The warranty record works the date out for you.

Maintenance wash

For our regular customers. Same standard, less work. Do what the car needs. Look hard for anything that needs extra.

Outside

  1. Wheels and tyres. They usually aren't that dirty. Dirty? Pressure wash up close to get the loose brake dust off, then look again.

    • Still needs hard work? Use the acid wheel cleaner. Never acid on chrome, polished, bare metal or coated wheels.
    • If not, the foam does it: the Green Star in it breaks the brake dust down.
    • Wheels bucket: 1 or 2 capfuls of Green Star, measured with the bottle's lid.
    How to do it

    Regulars' wheels usually aren't that dirty. If they are, pressure wash up close first to get the loose brake dust off, then look again. Still needs hard work? Acid cleaner, but never on chrome, polished, bare metal or coated wheels. If not, the foam is enough: the Green Star in it breaks the brake dust down. Wheels bucket: 1 or 2 capfuls of Green Star. Same brushes as the exterior detail: barrel, face, tyre, arch, corners, then rinse.

  2. Spray down the whole car fully. Too dirty for that? Do the full pre-wash from the exterior detail instead.

    How to do it

    Hose the whole car down, top to bottom, to knock the loose dirt off. If it's caked in mud or really grimy, this isn't enough: do the full pre-wash with foam and a rinse instead.

  3. Foam it up. Film the foam shot first, then go straight into the touch wash. Same method as the exterior touch wash.

    • Foam cannon: Green Star and the touch wash chemical, 1:3, 100 ml in total, the rest water.
    • Touch wash bucket: the touch wash chemical and water. No Green Star.
    How to do it

    Foam the car with the same foam cannon mix: 25 ml Green Star and 75 ml touch wash chemical (1:3), then top up with water. Film the foam shot and take the foam photo straight away. Wash top down, in straight lines.

  4. Rinse the whole car.

    How to do it

    Rinse top to bottom until no foam is left anywhere, mirrors and door edges too.

  5. Clay the front only, where the bugs are, plus the windscreen and mirrors.

    How to do it

    Regulars don't need a full clay. Do the bonnet, front bumper and grille where the bugs land, the windscreen and the mirrors. Soapy and wet, light pressure, side to side.

    How to do it

    How to do it

    Most regulars only need it topped up where it's worn, like the bonnet, roof and lower doors. If the water isn't beading anywhere, do the whole car. Towel wet from the pressure washer and folded in quarters, 3 sprays of sealant on it, 2 to 3 on the panel, wipe, rinse straight away.

  9. Dry, blower, glass and tyre shine, the same as the exterior detail.

    How to do it

    Dry with clean towels, blow out the gaps, clean the glass streak-free and dress the tyres with no sling. Exactly like steps 9 to 12 of the exterior detail.

  2. Front seats: the full clean.

  3. Back seats only where they need it. Look for dirty spots and clean them.

  5. Door jambs. Fairly clean? A wipe down with no chemical is enough. Dirty ones get the rinseless wash.

  6. Finish. Mats back in, steering wheel cover on. No review card or business card: they're regulars.

    How to do it

    Mats back in and straight, steering wheel cover on. No cards: regulars already have them, and we don't ask them for reviews.
