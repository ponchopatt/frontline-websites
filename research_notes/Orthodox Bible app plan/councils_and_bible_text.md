# Church Councils Reading Plans and Licensable Orthodox Bible Texts

Research date: 2026-10-16. Sources were fetched in this session unless marked "background knowledge, not verified this session". The reader should treat those background items as standard textbook facts that still need a check against a primary or Orthodox source before they ship in the app.

## Q1. What are the essential facts for each of the seven councils, and what are the best free primary texts for a reading plan?

### Takeaway
NPNF Series 2, Vol. 14 (Percival, 1900, public domain) gives free English texts of the creeds, definitions, canons and key letters of all seven councils. It also includes the canons of Trullo (692) and of the local synods that Orthodox canon law received: Ancyra, Neocaesarea, Gangra, Antioch, Laodicea, Sardica and Carthage. That is enough for a full canon-and-decree reading track. Orthodox count seven ecumenical councils, received by the whole Church. Catholics count 21, tied to papal ratification. They treat Constantinople 869–870 as the 8th and reject the Orthodox reception of Trullo and of the 879–880 council.

### Cited Findings
- **Free primary text, NPNF2 vol. 14.** CCEL hosts "The Seven Ecumenical Councils" (Schaff/Percival), covering Nicaea I, Constantinople I, Ephesus, Chalcedon, Constantinople II, Constantinople III and Nicaea II — [CCEL npnf214](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214)
- **Canons in the volume.** NPNF2-14 includes canons of Nicaea I, Constantinople I, Ephesus, Chalcedon, the Council in Trullo (Quinisext) and Nicaea II. It also has the local synods of Ancyra, Neocaesarea, Gangra, Antioch (in Encaeniis), Laodicea, Sardica and Carthage (the "Code of Canons of the African Church") — [CCEL NPNF2-14 TOC](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214/npnf214.toc.html)
- **New Advent version.** New Advent splits the same volume into one page per council. The Nicaea page (3801) has the Creed with the "Ecthesis of the Synod at Nice", Canons 1–20 as separate headings, and the Synodal Letter to the Church of Alexandria. The source line reads "Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, Vol. 14" — [New Advent 3801](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3801.htm)
- **Summary of the seven councils (OrthodoxWiki):**
  - I Nicaea (325): condemned Arianism, adopted the Nicene Creed.
  - II Constantinople (381): revised the Creed into the form used by Eastern and Oriental Orthodox.
  - III Ephesus (431): condemned Nestorianism, proclaimed Mary Theotokos.
  - IV Chalcedon (451): defined the two natures against Eutychianism/Monophysitism. Oriental Orthodox do not receive it.
  - V Constantinople II (553): reaffirmed the earlier councils and condemned Nestorian, Monophysite and "new Arian" writings.
  - VI Constantinople III (680–681): condemned Monothelitism and affirmed two wills in Christ.
  - VII Nicaea II (787): restored icon veneration.
  - Source: [OrthodoxWiki: Ecumenical Councils](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Ecumenical_Councils)
- **Quinisext/Trullo (692).** Mostly administrative and disciplinary. It raised some local canons to ecumenical status. Orthodox accept it as part of the Sixth Ecumenical Council, and Roman Catholics reject that — [OrthodoxWiki](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Ecumenical_Councils)
- **Council of 879–880 ("Eighth").** It restored St Photius, anathematized anyone who altered the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (relevant to the Filioque), and annulled the council of 869–870. The West first accepted it and later repudiated it. Some Orthodox call it ecumenical, while others see it as an important local council — [OrthodoxWiki](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Ecumenical_Councils); [OrthodoxWiki: Eighth Ecumenical Council](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Eighth_Ecumenical_Council)
- **Who supports calling 879–880 ecumenical.** Supporters include John Meyendorff and John Romanides, and some note that all five patriarchates were represented. The 1907 Catholic Encyclopedia calls it the "Pseudosynodus Photiana" — [Wikipedia: Fourth Council of Constantinople (Eastern Orthodox)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Council_of_Constantinople_(Eastern_Orthodox))
- **Palamite councils (1341–1351, "Ninth").** They affirmed hesychast theology (St Gregory Palamas, the essence/energies distinction) and condemned Barlaam of Calabria. Their ecumenical status is disputed within Orthodoxy — [OrthodoxWiki](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Ecumenical_Councils). Dr David Ford, speaking at the OCA's OTSA meeting, argued for recognising 879–880 as the 8th and 1341–1351 as the 9th — [OCA news](https://oca.org/news/headline-news/dr.-david-ford-addresses-otsa-annual-meeting)
- **How ecumenicity is decided.** In Roman teaching it depends on papal ratification. In the Orthodox "receptionist" view it depends on reception by the whole Church — [OrthodoxWiki](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Ecumenical_Councils)

### Inferences
Background knowledge, not verified this session. Check each item before publishing.

**I. Nicaea I (325)**
- Emperor: Constantine I.
- Present: Alexander of Alexandria with his deacon Athanasius, Hosius of Cordoba, Eustathius of Antioch, St Nicholas of Myra (by tradition), Spyridon (by tradition). Traditionally "318 Fathers".
- Heresy: Arius, who taught that the Son is a creature ("there was when he was not").
- Decrees: the Creed with *homoousios*, a uniform date for Pascha, and 20 canons.
- Scripture at stake: John 1:1–18, Philippians 2:5–11, Colossians 1:15–20, Hebrews 1, Proverbs 8:22 (the LXX "the Lord created me" was the Arian proof-text), John 14:28, John 10:30.
- Father readings: Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* and *Orations against the Arians*; Athanasius, *De Decretis* (defence of the Nicene definition), NPNF2 vol. 4.

**II. Constantinople I (381)**
- Emperor: Theodosius I.
- Present: Meletius of Antioch (president until his death), Gregory the Theologian (president for a time), Gregory of Nyssa, Nectarius. About 150 Fathers.
- Heresies: Macedonians/Pneumatomachi, who denied the Spirit's divinity, and Apollinarianism.
- Decrees: the enlarged Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed ("the Lord, the Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father…"), with no Filioque. Canon 3 gives Constantinople second rank after Rome. About 7 canons.
- Scripture at stake: John 15:26, Matthew 28:19, 1 Corinthians 2:10–12, Acts 5:3–4.
- Father readings: Gregory the Theologian, *Theological Orations* 27–31 (Oration 31 is on the Spirit); Basil, *On the Holy Spirit* (NPNF2 vol. 8).

**III. Ephesus (431)**
- Emperor: Theodosius II.
- President: Cyril of Alexandria. Nestorius was condemned. Celestine of Rome's legates arrived later.
- Heresy: Nestorianism, which refused the title Theotokos.
- Decrees: deposed Nestorius, approved Cyril's Second Letter to Nestorius, and appended the Twelve Anathemas (Third Letter). Canon 7 forbids composing "another faith" than Nicaea's (later used against the Filioque). 8 canons.
- Scripture at stake: John 1:14, Luke 1:35 and 1:43, Galatians 4:4.
- Father readings: Cyril's 2nd and 3rd letters to Nestorius, and the Formula of Reunion (433). All are in NPNF2-14.

**IV. Chalcedon (451)**
- Emperor: Marcian, with Empress Pulcheria.
- Present: Roman legates; Anatolius of Constantinople. About 520–630 bishops.
- Heresy: Eutyches; the 449 "Robber Synod" of Ephesus was annulled.
- Decrees: the Definition of the Faith ("in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation"), receiving Cyril's synodical letters and Leo's Tome. 27 or 28 canons. Canon 28 (equal privileges for New Rome) was rejected by Leo.
- Orthodox nuance: Orthodox read the Tome through Cyril, and acclamations held that "Leo taught as Cyril". Canon 28 is received in the East.
- Oriental Orthodox (Coptic, Armenian and others) reject Chalcedon. Modern dialogue statements, such as Chambésy 1990, call the difference largely terminological.

**V. Constantinople II (553)**
- Emperor: Justinian I.
- President: Eutychius of Constantinople. Pope Vigilius refused to attend and later assented.
- Decrees: condemned the "Three Chapters" (Theodore of Mopsuestia, certain writings of Theodoret and Ibas), and issued 14 anathemas; Origenist anathemas are attributed to the council.
- No disciplinary canons.

**VI. Constantinople III (680–681)**
- Emperor: Constantine IV.
- Present: Patriarch George; legates of Pope Agatho, whose letter was received.
- Heresy: Monothelitism and Monoenergism.
- Decrees: two wills and two energies in Christ. Pope Honorius was anathematised among the Monothelites (awkward for Catholic apologetics).
- Confessor to read: St Maximus the Confessor, who died in 662, before the council.
- Scripture at stake: Matthew 26:39 and John 6:38 (Gethsemane).
- No canons; its canons came in 692 at Trullo (102 canons).

**VII. Nicaea II (787)**
- Rulers: Empress Irene with Constantine VI.
- President: Patriarch Tarasius, with Roman legates of Hadrian I.
- Heresy: Iconoclasm; the 754 iconoclast council of Hieria was rejected.
- Decrees: the Horos on veneration (*proskynesis*), distinct from worship (*latreia*), which belongs to God alone; "the honour paid to the image passes to the prototype" (Basil). 22 canons.
- Scripture at stake: Exodus 20:4 against Exodus 25:18–22 (the cherubim), Numbers 21:8–9, John 1:14, Colossians 1:15.
- Father reading: John of Damascus, *Three Treatises on the Divine Images*.
- Later: Triumph of Orthodoxy (843), the Synodikon of Orthodoxy (still read on the first Sunday of Lent).

**Catholic count of the councils (background)**
- Catholics count Constantinople IV (869–870), which deposed Photius, as the 8th ecumenical council, and continue to 21 (Vatican II).
- Catholics also count Lyons II (1274) and Florence (1438–45). Orthodox reject both, and Mark of Ephesus opposed Florence.
- The app should label 869–870 as "rejected by the Orthodox; annulled by the council of 879–880".

**Suggested day-by-day plan template (example: Nicaea, 7 days)**
1. Background and heresy: Arius's letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia, from Theodoret's *Ecclesiastical History* in NPNF2 vol. 3.
2. Scripture: John 1:1–18 with Proverbs 8 (LXX).
3. Scripture: Philippians 2:5–11, Colossians 1, Hebrews 1.
4. Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §§1–10.
5. *On the Incarnation* §§11–32.
6. The Creed and the Ecthesis (NPNF2-14), with the Synodal Letter.
7. Selected canons: 1, 5, 6, 7, 20, and 18 (on deacons). Also the Pascha rule.

Repeat the pattern for the other councils: heresy, then Scripture, then Fathers, then decree, then canons. For a plan on Trullo or the local councils, follow the NPNF2-14 canons with Balsamon/Zonaras notes; the NPNF "excursus" material carries Anglican scholarly commentary, so flag it. The Pedalion/Rudder (D. Cummings translation, 1957) is the classic Orthodox commentary, but the translation's copyright is unclear.

**Notes on the source edition**
- NPNF2-14 was edited by an Anglican, H. R. Percival, in 1900, and its notes sometimes reflect Western views.
- The app could show the primary texts only, without Percival's notes, or add its own Orthodox commentary.
- The 879–880 and Palamite councils are not in NPNF2-14. Free English translations of the 1351 Tome were not located in this session (see Gaps).

## Q2. What is the copyright or licence status of each candidate English Bible text, and which combination gives a complete Orthodox canon for free?

### Takeaway
The only fully free and redistributable texts are public-domain ones:
- **Brenton's English Septuagint**, the LXX Old Testament with apocrypha. It is LXX-based and so the best match for Orthodox use.
- **The World English Bible (WEB) with deuterocanon**. It is public domain and includes 3 Maccabees, 4 Maccabees, 1 Esdras, 2 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasses and Psalm 151. Its Old Testament is translated from the Masoretic text, but it supplies the books Orthodox need.
- **The KJV with Apocrypha**, which is public domain outside the UK.

Everything Orthodox-specific is copyrighted: the Orthodox Study Bible (OSB), NETS, the Lexham English Septuagint (LES), the Eastern Orthodox Bible (EOB) and the Holy Transfiguration Monastery (HTM) Psalter. The free choice is **Brenton for the Old Testament plus the WEB New Testament (or the KJV New Testament)**, with the Greek Patriarchal Text (1904) as a free Greek New Testament.

The eBible copyright page also states 1851 and public domain — [eBible Brenton copr](https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/copr.htm). ### Inferences
**Recommended free stack**
- **Old Testament:** Brenton (LXX, public domain, Orthodox book order). The language is archaic ("thee/thou") and some names are Greek forms ("Esaias"), so consider a light modernisation. A modernised edition cannot be called "Brenton" in a misleading way, but there is no trademark issue.
- **Optional parallel Old Testament:** the WEB deuterocanon gives a modern-English option for Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Maccabees and the rest. Its protocanonical Old Testament follows the Masoretic text and English versification.
- **New Testament:** WEB (modern, public domain; its base is the Majority Text, close to the Byzantine/Patriarchal text, from background knowledge) or KJV (Textus Receptus, Byzantine-family). Both are close to the Orthodox Patriarchal Text.
- **Greek:** the Patriarchal Text NT (Robinson files) and Swete or the 1935 Rahlfs for the LXX.

**Coverage of the Orthodox canon**
- The Orthodox canon adds Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch with the Epistle of Jeremiah, 1–3 Maccabees, 1 Esdras ("2 Esdras" in Greek usage, confusingly), the Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151, and Greek Esther and Daniel. 4 Maccabees is an appendix in Greek Bibles. The Slavonic tradition also includes 3 Esdras (Latin 4 Ezra).
- The WEB's 15 deuterocanonical books cover all of these, including 4 Maccabees and 2 Esdras.

**Upgrade path**
- If budget allows, license the OSB (NKJV plus the St Athanasius Academy Septuagint, the most widely used Orthodox English Bible) or the EOB from their rights holders.
- Approach the EOB editor first; the existing parish permission suggests he may be open to a free Orthodox app.

**Other languages (background, not verified)**
- Church Slavonic: Elizabeth Bible (1751), public domain by age; digital versions exist, for example in SWORD.
- Russian Synodal (1876): public domain.
- Romanian: Anania Bible (2001), copyrighted.
- Modern Greek: Vamvas, public domain by age; the Hellenic Bible Society's translations are copyrighted.
- Arabic: Van Dyck, public domain.

## Q3. What versification and Psalm-numbering mapping data exists, and how should an app map references?

### Takeaway
Use LXX numbering as the primary scheme for Orthodox users and show Hebrew/English numbers in brackets, for example "Ps 22 (23)". Map references with the CC BY 4.0 STEPBible TVTMS dataset, which reversifies between English, Hebrew, Greek and Latin traditions. SWORD/CrossWire supplies ready versification schemes ("LXX", "Orthodox", "Synodal"), though its mapping layer is incomplete.

### Cited Findings
- **Psalm number shifts (Hebrew to Greek):**
  - Hebrew 9 and 10 become Greek 9.
  - Hebrew 114 and 115 become Greek 113.
  - Hebrew 116 is split into Greek 114 and 115.
  - Hebrew 147 is split into Greek 146 and 147.
  - Verse-level: Hebrew 10 = Greek 9:21–38, and Hebrew 115 = Greek 113:9–26.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia: Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms); [Church Mother of God: Septuagint numbering](https://churchmotherofgod.org/terminologies/septuagint-numbering-of-psalms/); [Metropolitan Cantor Institute](https://metropolitancantorinstitute.org/liturgy/Psalms.html)
- **TVTMS (STEPBible).** It records versification differences in the Hebrew, Latin and Greek Old Testament traditions and in the early New Testament, against an English standard (NRSV, "virtually identical to KJV"). Its rules are simple, for example "if this chapter has 29 verses, it is using Greek versification", so a whole Bible can be reversified. Licence: CC BY 4.0, crediting "STEP Bible" with a link to www.STEPBible.org — [GitHub STEPBible-Data](https://github.com/STEPBible/STEPBible-Data)
- **SWORD versification schemes.** These include KJV, KJVA, NRSV(A), MT, Leningrad, Synodal (1.6.1), Vulg, Catholic/Catholic2, LXX (1.7.2, for the Greek Septuagint and its translations), Orthodox (1.7.2, LXX-based, with a different book order and fewer books), SynodalProt and others.
  - Mapping between schemes ("av11n mapping") was planned but the wiki does not describe it as complete. Harry Plantinga's mapping has errors.
  - When importing into a scheme that doesn't fit, extra verses fold into the preceding verse.
  - Source: [CrossWire wiki: Alternate Versification](https://wiki.crosswire.org/Alternate_Versification)

### Inferences
**The full Psalm table**
- Psalms 1–8 have the same numbers.
- Hebrew 11–113 = Greek 10–112, so LXX = MT − 1.
- Hebrew 117–146 = Greek 116–145.
- Hebrew 148–150 have the same numbers.
- Psalm 151 exists only in the LXX.

**Psalm verse numbers**
- Verse numbers often differ by one, because the LXX and the Hebrew number the superscription as verse 1 while English Bibles do not. Brenton follows this, as eBible's sample "Psalm 23:1" = English 24:1 with its title suggests. This is an inference from the sample, not verified.

**Other books with LXX/MT differences (background)**
- Jeremiah: the LXX order differs, with the oracles against the nations (MT 46–51) placed after 25:13, and the LXX is about one-eighth shorter.
- Exodus 35–40: different order.
- 3 and 4 Kingdoms (= 1 and 2 Kings), and 1 and 2 Kingdoms (= 1 and 2 Samuel).
- Proverbs 24–31: different order.
- Esther and Daniel: Greek additions; in Daniel, Susanna and Bel are separate or numbered chapters 13–14.
- Malachi 4 (English) = Malachi 3:19–24 (LXX/Hebrew).
- Joel 2:28–32 (English) = Joel 3 (LXX/Hebrew).
- Romans 16:25–27: in Byzantine manuscripts, and thus the Patriarchal Text, it sits at 14:24–26.

**Data model**
- Store each text in its native versification: Brenton = LXX, WEB/KJV = KJVA-like.
- Keep a canonical internal key (for example OSIS book plus chapter and verse in the LXX scheme), with a TVTMS-derived mapping table to KJV/NRSV.
- Reading-plan references can then be written once and resolved to any translation.
- Let users toggle "Show Hebrew numbering".
- Book names: use Orthodox names in the UI ("1 Kingdoms", "Sirach / Wisdom of Sirach", "Psalm 50 (51)") with English aliases for search.

**Lectionary**
- Orthodox lectionary references (Apostol and Gospel) use NT versification, which is the same as KJV apart from minor cases like Romans 16.
- Old Testament (Paremia) readings use LXX references.
