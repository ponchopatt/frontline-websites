Hard sites

We love
the difficult
blocks.

Most homes in bushfire country sit on a slope, where a standard slab, brick veneer and tiled roof won't work or costs far too much. We build pole homes and Spantec steel floors on steep ground, bushfire homes from BAL 12.5 right up to flame zone, and homes on flood-affected, narrow and remote blocks. We've lived in the Hawkesbury all our lives, and we've seen firsthand what a bushfire does to a house.

How it starts

Concept first.
Contract last.

Our advice: don't sign a building contract until council has approved the plans and your builder is ready to start.

01

Call Luke

He can usually look up the address on Street View while you're on the phone and tell you straight away whether, say, a second storey will work. If not, he'll visit the site. There's no fee for this.

02

A concept plan

A basic plan drawn to scale, not full council drawings. It starts with a written brief: your budget, a must list, a don't list and a wish list.

03

An exact price

We price the concept at no cost. Every quote is specific to your site, with all site costs included and no hidden costs. We can price alternative materials or methods too.

04

Approval, then contract

Council approval comes first, and we can lodge it for you at no fee. Then you sign the MBA BC4 fixed price contract, the HBCF cover goes in and we start building.

Before you call

Questions Luke gets asked.

Can we stay in the house during a second storey addition?

Most clients do. The crew comes in via the scaffold and through the roof, not your front door. To be honest, we'll likely kill your grass storing materials and we'll make noise early in the morning. But we clean up daily and keep a safe, secure way into your home.

Is my house strong enough to build on top of?

Most homes are, but a builder should check. Homes sitting on brick piers only, or in very poor condition, may not suit. Call Luke with the address. He can usually tell from Street View straight away, or he'll come out to the site.

Can you build on a bushfire-prone or steep block?

Yes. We build from BAL 12.5 right through to flame zone, and on steep ground we use pole homes and Spantec steel floor systems. Flame zone adds real cost, so it pays to plan for it at the concept stage rather than after.

Can I supply some of the materials myself?

Yes. There are no restrictions on suppliers, and owners can supply items like tiles, bathroom fittings or appliances directly. Ask first how it affects insurance, warranties and the construction program.

Do you charge extra to travel?

No. Our rates are the same for every area, whether you're building at Pitt Town, Penrith or Katoomba.

What contract do you use?

The Master Builders Association BC4 fixed price building contract, or an MBA NSW cost plus contract. Don't sign any building contract until council approval is in hand.
